Specialised Investment Funds: The New Middle Ground Between Mutual Funds and PMS

For years, Indian investors faced an awkward gap. Mutual funds started at ₹500 but were confined to long-only strategies. Portfolio management services started at ₹50 lakh, and alternative investment funds at ₹1 crore. Anything in between had nowhere to go. A specialised investment fund, or SIF, is SEBI's answer to that gap, and in its first year it has grown faster than most expected.

By SELEQT Editorial

Since the first SIF launched in September 2025, seventeen fund houses have entered the category, and AMFI data showed industry assets above ₹38,000 crore by September 2026. That growth makes it worth understanding what these funds can do, what they cannot, and who they are actually for.

What a SIF is

A SIF is a new product category that sits inside the mutual fund framework. It is launched by an existing asset management company, regulated under the mutual fund regulations, priced daily at a net asset value, and held in the same way as a mutual fund unit. What changes is the investment freedom the fund manager is given.

The most important freedom is the ability to take unhedged short positions of up to 25% of net assets using exchange-traded derivatives. A conventional mutual fund can only profit when the securities it owns go up. A SIF can also profit when securities it considers overvalued go down, and can reduce its net exposure to the market when the manager is cautious.

SEBI's framework permits a defined set of strategies, including equity long-short funds, equity long-short funds focused outside the largest companies, sector rotation funds, debt long-short funds, sectoral debt funds, active asset allocators and hybrid long-short funds. Each SIF must declare its strategy and stay within it.

The ₹10 lakh threshold

The minimum investment is ₹10 lakh per investor, per AMC, measured across all of that fund house's SIF strategies combined. Investors can reach the threshold through a lump sum or through systematic investments, provided the total crosses ₹10 lakh. Accredited investors are exempt from the minimum.

The threshold is deliberate. These strategies are more complex than a standard equity fund, and a short position can lose more than expected if the market moves sharply against it. SEBI's intention was to keep SIFs within reach of serious investors without opening them to everyone.

SIF vs PMS: the structural difference

On the surface, both offer a professionally managed portfolio with more flexibility than a mutual fund. Underneath, they are built very differently.

A PMS holds securities directly in your own demat account. Every time the manager sells a stock at a profit, the gain is taxed in your hands that year. A high-turnover PMS can therefore produce a substantial annual tax bill even when you have not withdrawn a rupee.

A SIF is a pooled fund. Trades happen inside the fund, and you are taxed only when you redeem your units, in the same way as a mutual fund investor. For an active strategy, that deferral can add meaningfully to the return you keep, for the reasons we set out in your real return after tax and inflation.

The trade-off is customisation. A PMS can be tailored to exclude particular stocks or sectors, or to manage an existing portfolio you bring in. A SIF is the same for every investor in it.

SIF vs AIF

Category III AIFs have offered long-short and hedge-style strategies for years, but with a ₹1 crore minimum and tax computed at the fund level, often at the highest marginal rate. A SIF brings some of the same tools into a lower-cost, lower-minimum structure that is taxed like a mutual fund. For many investors who were curious about long-short investing but unwilling to commit ₹1 crore to it, the SIF is the more sensible entry point.

How SIFs are taxed

Because SIFs operate within the mutual fund framework, they are generally taxed in the same way. Funds that keep at least 65% in domestic equity are typically treated as equity-oriented, with long-term gains after twelve months taxed at 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh. Debt-oriented strategies are taxed at slab rate. Hybrid strategies need to be checked individually, because their classification depends on the portfolio mix.

The detail matters, and it is worth confirming the tax classification in the offer document before investing rather than assuming it.

What to be careful about

Liquidity can be lower. Some SIFs offer redemption only on certain days rather than daily, and SEBI allows notice periods of up to 15 working days. Money that may be needed suddenly should not be here.

Track records are short. Most SIFs are little more than a year old. The manager's experience running similar strategies elsewhere is a better guide than the SIF's own history, which is too brief to judge.

Shorting is a skill, not a feature. A long-short strategy is only as good as the manager's ability to identify overvalued securities and size positions sensibly. In a strongly rising market, a fund with short positions will often lag a plain index fund. That is the cost of the protection, and investors should expect it.

Read the strategy, not the label. Two SIFs in the same category can behave very differently depending on how much net exposure they run. Ask what the fund's typical net equity exposure has been, and what the range is allowed to be.

Who a SIF suits

A SIF fits an investor with a long horizon, a portfolio already built on core equity and debt holdings, and an interest in strategies that behave differently from the market. It can serve as a diversifier within the equity allocation, or as a more tax-efficient alternative to an active PMS for investors who do not need customisation.

It does not suit someone looking for a higher-return version of a large-cap fund, or anyone who will be uncomfortable when the fund trails the index during a strong rally.

Where this leaves you

SIFs fill a real gap in the Indian market, combining some of the flexibility of hedge-style strategies with the tax and cost structure of a mutual fund. They are a tool, not an upgrade. Used deliberately as part of a portfolio, they can earn their place. Bought because they are new, they are just another product.

Frequently asked questions

What is a specialised investment fund?
A SIF is a SEBI-regulated product category launched by mutual fund houses. It allows strategies such as long-short equity, with unhedged short positions of up to 25% of net assets. It sits between mutual funds and PMS in complexity and minimum investment.
What is the minimum investment in a SIF?
₹10 lakh per investor, measured across all SIF strategies of the same AMC. It can be reached through a lump sum or systematic investments. Accredited investors are exempt.
How are SIFs taxed?
Generally like mutual funds. Equity-oriented SIFs follow equity capital gains rules, and debt-oriented ones are taxed at slab rate. Check the classification in the offer document for hybrid strategies.
Is a SIF better than a PMS?
Neither is better in general. A SIF defers tax until redemption and has a lower minimum. A PMS offers customisation and direct ownership of securities, but trades are taxed in your hands as they happen.
Are SIFs risky?
They can be. Short positions and derivatives add risks that a long-only fund does not carry, and liquidity may be lower. They suit experienced investors with a long horizon.

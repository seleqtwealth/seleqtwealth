    Before you begin How to use this file

    A map of what you hold, where it is, and who to call. Five things decide whether it is useful on the day it is needed.

    1. Fill it in slowly. Nobody completes this in one sitting. Start with banking, insurance and the location of original documents, which is what a family needs first, and come back to the rest.
    2. Never write live credentials. No full passwords, no PINs, no card numbers, no CVVs, no OTP details. Record where the information lives (a password manager, a locker, a named person) rather than the information itself.
    3. Store it the way you would store a will. A home safe, a bank locker, or an encrypted file with a passphrase your spouse or executor already knows. A copy sitting in an email inbox is not storage.
    4. Tell two people it exists. A perfectly completed file that nobody can find has no value. Name a spouse, an adult child, or an executor, and tell them where it is and how to open it.
    5. Review it once a year. Also after a marriage, a birth, a death, a property purchase, a change of residency status, or the sale of a business. Log each review on the final page.

    A word on security. This file is valuable to your family precisely because it would also be valuable to someone else. Treat it as a map, not a set of keys. Every field in this kit is designed so that a person who finds it cannot transact with it, but a person you have briefed can act on it within a day.

    What this is not. This kit is an organiser, not a legal instrument. It does not transfer assets, it does not override a will, and completing it is not a substitute for a properly drafted will, a trust deed, or up to date nominations. Where this file surfaces a gap, that is the point of it. Bring the gap to your adviser.

    How this page works. Your answers are kept only in this browser tab while you work.

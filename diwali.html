Deepavali 2026

Shubh Deepavali

Five small things to play with this Diwali, made with care by the SELEQT team. Turn your sound on, find a quiet moment, and light something.

Raag of Lamps Strings of Light Sparkler Writing Fireworks over Sabarmati Gujarat After Dark
01

Raag of Lamps

Tap the river to set a diya afloat. Each one sings a note of Raag Yaman, the evening raga, over a soft tanpura, lower to the left and higher to the right. Leave them be, and they drift downstream and slowly fall into rhythm with one another.

Tap the water to float a diya

Diya photograph by Abhishek Soni and river by Mike Iukhtenko, on Unsplash.

02

Strings of Light

Strings of lights hang before the tree of life jali of the Sidi Saiyyed Mosque, strung like a sitar. Sweep your finger or cursor across them: each string sings a note of the same raga, and the light ripples along it as it sounds.

Sweep across the lights

Tree of life jali, Sidi Saiyyed Mosque, Ahmedabad. Photo: Bernard Gagnon, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0; cropped, converted to monochrome and relit.

03

Sparkler Writing

Press and draw with a phuljhadi on the riverfront at night. Write a name, a wish, a shape: it lingers like a long-exposure photograph. Save the picture and send it home.

Press and draw

Background: the Atal Bridge by Sahil Parmar on Unsplash, defocused.

04

Fireworks over Sabarmati

The Atal Bridge on the Sabarmati, Ahmedabad, on Diwali night. Tap the sky to send up a shell and watch it bloom over the riverfront, light the clouds and shimmer in the river.

Tap the sky

Photograph of the Atal Bridge by Sahil Parmar on Unsplash.

05

Gujarat After Dark

Every Diwali, a picture of India "lit up from space" does the rounds. It isn't Diwali: NASA explains it is a 2003 composite of weather-satellite data, coloured by the year each light first appeared, and that Diwali's extra light is "likely imperceptible" from orbit. What the satellites really show is better: how much brighter Gujarat has grown at night. Economists read night lights as a measure of growth.

How much brighter, 2012 to 2026

    Each frame is the median of about 45 nights from January to March, after Diwali, so festival lighting never inflates a year. The brightest city cores reach the satellite's ceiling, so their growth is understated; Ahmedabad is measured across its metro for that reason.

On night lights and growth: Henderson, Storeygard and Weil, American Economic Review, 2012. Brightness is not income; this is a picture of change, not a forecast.

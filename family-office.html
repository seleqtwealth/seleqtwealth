Family Office

Everything a family owns, decides, and intends to pass on, held together by one office. SELEQT helps families design and run that office, in India and across borders.

A family office is the private team and structure that runs a family's wealth as one balance sheet: investments, tax, legal structures, protection, succession, and the governance that keeps a family aligned across generations.

Most wealthy families already have the pieces: a private banker, a chartered accountant, a lawyer, an insurance adviser, a handful of fund managers. What is usually missing is someone who owns the whole picture.

A family office closes that gap. It sets one investment policy, sees every asset in one report, and is designed so a tax decision in Dubai does not quietly undo a succession plan in Mumbai. India's family offices grew from about 45 in 2018 to more than 300 by 2024 (PwC India).

The work

What a family office actually does

Investment Office

One investment policy for the whole family: allocation, manager selection and monitoring across listed markets, private equity, credit and real estate.

Tax & Structuring

Holding structures, residency planning and tax efficiency across every country the family lives, earns or invests in.

Succession & Governance

Family charter, trusts and wills, and a clear way for the family to make decisions together across generations.

Consolidated Reporting

Every account, entity and asset in one view: performance, risk, cash flow and tax, on one page, on one date.

Risk & Protection

Insurance, key-person cover, asset protection and liquidity planning, so the unexpected need not force a bad sale.

Next Generation & Giving

Preparing heirs to own wealth well, and structuring philanthropy that carries the family's values forward.

The set-up

How a family office is built

Every family is different, but the sequence rarely is. We take families through it end to end.

  1. Map the balance sheet

    Every asset, entity, account and liability in every country: who owns what, how it is held, and what it costs.

  2. Agree the family's rules

    A family charter: who decides, how disagreements are settled, and what each generation can expect.

  3. Design the structure

    The right vehicles, such as a holding company, LLP, private trust or a GIFT City fund, and where each should sit.

  4. Write the investment policy

    Return goals, risk limits, liquidity needs and the asset mix the office will run to.

  5. Build the plumbing

    Entities, bank and custody accounts, reporting, and the right specialists in each country.

  6. Run and review

    Disciplined execution and a standing review, so the office keeps pace as the family changes.

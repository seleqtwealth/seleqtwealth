The centre

What GIFT City is

Gujarat International Finance Tec-City, India's first International Financial Services Centre.

One regulator. The International Financial Services Centres Authority (IFSCA), created by an Act of Parliament in 2019, oversees banking, funds, capital markets and insurance in the centre.

Dollar based. Business is done in US dollars and other foreign currencies, and for exchange control purposes the centre is treated as outside India. Money moves in and out in your currency.

Built for neutrality. For non-residents, India exempts many GIFT City returns, and trades on its exchanges carry no securities transaction tax or stamp duty.

Already at scale. Around 400 funds, more than 35 Indian and international banks, and its own exchanges, NSE IX and India INX.

How it works from where you live

The same investment can be efficient for one family and costly for another. Choose where you are resident to see what works well, how you invest, and what to watch for.

Investing from Monaco

Monaco generally levies no personal income tax on its residents, so India's own exemptions for non-residents do most of the work. That makes GIFT City's dollar products unusually clean for a Monaco-based family, with two important exceptions.

Tax treaty with India

No double tax treaty. India and Monaco have a tax information exchange agreement, in force since 2013, and both share account information under the OECD Common Reporting Standard.

What works well

  • US dollar fixed deposits at GIFT City banks
  • GIFT City funds investing in India or global markets
  • A managed portfolio account, from USD 75,000
  • A Family Investment Fund for larger family pools

How you invest

  • Choose the product: a deposit, a fund, a managed account or a family fund.
  • Complete KYC with the GIFT City bank or manager: passport, address proof, tax residence and source of funds.
  • Sign the account or subscription documents.
  • Wire US dollars or another accepted currency from your bank abroad.

Tax points

  • Monaco generally does not tax residents' investment income.
  • India exempts many GIFT City returns for non-residents, such as interest on GIFT City bank deposits and income from qualifying GIFT City funds.
  • With no treaty, those Indian exemptions are what protect you, so the choice of product matters.

Watch for

  • French nationals living in Monaco are generally taxed by France as if resident there, under the 1963 convention. Use the Europe route.
  • US citizens remain US taxpayers wherever they live. Use the United States route.
  • Account details are reported to the authorities under the Common Reporting Standard.

Investing from the European Union

France, Germany, Italy and most EU states have full tax treaties with India. You are taxed at home on worldwide income, usually with credit for Indian tax paid, so the aim is efficient structures that report cleanly at home.

Tax treaty with India

Yes, with most EU states, including France, Germany and Italy. India and France signed an amending protocol in February 2026.

What works well

  • Dollar or euro deposits at GIFT City banks
  • Bonds and listed securities, held directly
  • A segregated managed portfolio account
  • GIFT City funds, where you qualify as a professional investor

How you invest

  • Choose the product: a deposit, a fund, a managed account or a family fund.
  • Complete KYC with the GIFT City bank or manager: passport, address proof, tax residence and source of funds.
  • Sign the account or subscription documents.
  • Wire US dollars or another accepted currency from your bank abroad.

Tax points

  • Income and gains are generally taxed at home: France's flat tax on investment income, for example, is 31.4% from 2026.
  • Treaty relief and foreign tax credits stop the same income being taxed twice.
  • India's non-resident exemptions reduce what is taxed in India in the first place.

Watch for

  • EU rules (PRIIPs) require a Key Information Document before a packaged product is offered to retail investors, and most GIFT City funds do not have one.
  • Your home country's rules on foreign funds can treat gains as income.
  • Accounts are reported to your home tax authority under the Common Reporting Standard.

Investing from the United Kingdom

The UK and India have a long-standing tax treaty. Since April 2025 the UK taxes residents on worldwide income and gains as they arise, so a fund's UK tax status matters as much as its return.

Tax treaty with India

Yes. The 1993 convention, amended by a 2013 protocol.

What works well

  • US dollar deposits at GIFT City banks
  • Bonds and listed securities, held directly
  • A segregated managed portfolio account
  • Funds that hold UK reporting fund status

How you invest

  • Choose the product: a deposit, a fund, a managed account or a family fund.
  • Complete KYC with the GIFT City bank or manager: passport, address proof, tax residence and source of funds.
  • Sign the account or subscription documents.
  • Wire US dollars or another accepted currency from your bank abroad.

Tax points

  • Interest is taxed as income in the UK.
  • Gains on a fund with UK reporting status are capital gains.
  • Gains on a non-reporting offshore fund are taxed as income, at income tax rates.

Watch for

  • Most GIFT City funds are not UK reporting funds, so check before you invest.
  • The remittance basis ended on 6 April 2025.
  • New arrivals may qualify for the four-year foreign income and gains regime, which rewards careful timing.

Investing from the United States

US citizens and green card holders are taxed on worldwide income wherever they live. India through GIFT City still works, but pooled funds are usually the wrong wrapper.

Tax treaty with India

Yes. A full income tax treaty, and a FATCA agreement in force since 2015.

What works well

  • Indian and global securities, held directly
  • A segregated managed portfolio account
  • US dollar deposits at GIFT City banks

How you invest

  • Choose the product: a deposit, a fund, a managed account or a family fund.
  • Complete KYC with the GIFT City bank or manager: passport, address proof, tax residence and source of funds.
  • Sign the account or subscription documents.
  • Wire US dollars or another accepted currency from your bank abroad.

Tax points

  • Most non-US pooled funds are PFICs, taxed heavily in the US unless an election is available.
  • Deposit interest is ordinary income in the US.
  • Where India exempts the income, there is little Indian tax to credit, so the US rate is the rate you pay.

Watch for

  • Many GIFT City funds do not accept US persons.
  • Form 8621 for each PFIC, FBAR above USD 10,000, and Form 8938 above its thresholds.
  • Every account is reported to the IRS under FATCA.

Investing from the UAE and the Gulf

The UAE levies no personal income tax, and India and the UAE have a tax treaty. With India's non-resident exemptions, GIFT City's dollar products can be highly efficient on both sides.

Tax treaty with India

Yes. A comprehensive treaty, amended in 2007. Treaty claims need a UAE tax residency certificate and India's Form 10F.

What works well

  • US dollar deposits at GIFT City banks
  • GIFT City funds investing in India or global markets
  • A managed portfolio account, from USD 75,000
  • A Family Investment Fund for the wider family

How you invest

  • Choose the product: a deposit, a fund, a managed account or a family fund.
  • Complete KYC with the GIFT City bank or manager: passport, address proof, tax residence and source of funds.
  • Sign the account or subscription documents.
  • Wire US dollars or another accepted currency from your bank abroad.

Tax points

  • No personal income tax in the UAE.
  • India exempts many GIFT City returns for non-residents.
  • Keep a tax residency certificate and Form 10F on file for any treaty claim in India.

Watch for

  • A UAE certificate issued on the shorter domestic test may not support a treaty claim in India.
  • India's own residence rules, based on days spent in India and Indian income, can change your status. Track them every year.
  • Other Gulf states have their own, different treaties with India.

Investing from Singapore

Singapore generally does not tax capital gains, and India and Singapore have a tax treaty. Since 2017, though, India taxes gains on Indian shares bought by Singapore residents, so structure still matters.

Tax treaty with India

Yes. The 1994 treaty, amended in 2016 so that India taxes gains on Indian shares acquired from 1 April 2017.

What works well

  • GIFT City funds and US dollar deposits
  • Direct Indian exposure held by a Singapore family office
  • A GIFT City Family Investment Fund alongside a Singapore office

How you invest

  • Choose the product: a deposit, a fund, a managed account or a family fund.
  • Complete KYC with the GIFT City bank or manager: passport, address proof, tax residence and source of funds.
  • Sign the account or subscription documents.
  • Wire US dollars or another accepted currency from your bank abroad.

Tax points

  • Singapore generally does not tax capital gains.
  • India taxes gains on Indian shares acquired since April 2017.
  • India exempts many GIFT City returns for non-residents.

Watch for

  • Assuming the pre-2017 exemption on Indian share gains still applies.
  • Singapore's family office incentives (13O and 13U) carry minimum size, staffing and local spending conditions.

For Indians living abroad

Non-resident Indians can save in rupees through NRE and NRO accounts, or in dollars through GIFT City. Each has a place, and the right mix depends on where you live and whether you plan to return.

Tax treaty with India

Depends on where you live. Choose your country of residence above for the treaty position.

What works well

  • GIFT City dollar deposits: dollar savings with no rupee risk
  • GIFT City funds: India exposure, held in dollars
  • NRE accounts: rupee savings that stay fully repatriable

How you invest

  • Choose the product: a deposit, a fund, a managed account or a family fund.
  • Complete KYC with the GIFT City bank or manager: passport, address proof, tax residence and source of funds.
  • Sign the account or subscription documents.
  • Wire US dollars or another accepted currency from your bank abroad.

Tax points

  • Interest on NRE accounts is exempt in India for non-residents.
  • Interest on GIFT City bank deposits is exempt for non-residents, and also while you are 'not ordinarily resident' after returning.
  • NRO interest is taxable in India, and your country of residence may tax all of it.

Watch for

  • NRO repatriation is capped at USD 1 million per financial year.
  • NRE savings carry rupee risk against your home currency.
  • US-based NRIs face PFIC rules on Indian and GIFT City funds alike.
